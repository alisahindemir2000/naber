Naber: Privacy Policy & Terms of Use

Effective date: November 1, 2026

In short
  • Naber is a simple instant messaging app that works only over Bluetooth between phones that are close to each other.
  • It does not use the internet, has no servers and no accounts.
  • It does not collect, sell or share any of your data.
  • The developer cannot see your messages and does not check or control them.
  • You alone are responsible for everything you write and send.

1. Who this applies to

This document covers the Naber mobile app (the "App"), published by its developer ("we", "us"). By installing or using the App and tapping "Accept and continue", you agree to this Privacy Policy and Terms of Use. If you do not agree, do not use the App and uninstall it.

2. Data we do not collect

The App has no internet access permission. We do not run any server. We do not collect, receive, store, sell or share any personal data. There are no user accounts, no phone number or email sign-up, no advertising, no analytics, no crash reporting, no tracking and no third-party SDKs that send data anywhere. We never receive your messages, contacts, name, location or any other information.

3. Data stored only on your phone

To work, the App keeps the following on your own device: the display name you choose, a random identifier created for this installation, the people you have added, your messages, your dating profile (if you turn dating mode on), your blocked list and your settings. This data stays in the App's private storage on your phone and is saved in encrypted form. It is not backed up to us and we have no access to it. Uninstalling the App or clearing its data deletes it permanently.

4. What nearby people can see

While you are visible, Naber users within Bluetooth range can see your display name and the random identifier of this installation. If you turn dating mode on, other dating users nearby can also see your dating nickname and short bio. People you chat with receive the messages you send them, and those messages are then stored on their phone, outside our and your control. Removing a chat or using Unmatch asks the other phone to delete it too, but this cannot be guaranteed (for example, someone may take a screenshot). Do not send anything you would not want the other person to keep.

5. Bluetooth and permissions

The App asks for Bluetooth permissions (scan, advertise and connect) only to find nearby Naber users and exchange messages with them. On some older Android versions, the system requires location permission for Bluetooth scanning; the App does not read, store or share your location. You can turn invisible mode on at any time to stop Bluetooth completely, and you can revoke permissions in your phone's settings.

6. Encryption and security

Messages travel directly from phone to phone over Bluetooth and are end-to-end encrypted. You can compare a verification code with family and friends to make sure you are talking to the right person. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee that data on your device or another person's device will never be accessed, lost or disclosed (for example, if a phone is lost, unlocked or compromised).
